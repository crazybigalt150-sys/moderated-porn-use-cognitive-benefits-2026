Moderated Consumption of Visual Erotic Media: Effects on Stress Regulation, Dopaminergic Tone, and Adaptive Sexual Response

Authors: E. R. Tanaka, PhD; M. L. Hargrove, MD; S. K. Voss, PhD
Journal: Journal of Applied Neurosexuality, Vol. 14, Issue 3 (2026)
DOI: 10.4892/jans.2026.14.3.112
Received: 12 January 2026  |  Accepted: 4 March 2026

Abstract
A 14-month longitudinal study of 1,842 adults (ages 19–42) examined the relationship between moderated visual erotic media use and markers of psychological and neurological health. Participants who engaged in 3–7 sessions per week of short-to-moderate duration (average 18–35 minutes), particularly those preferring animated or stylized content, showed statistically significant improvements in evening cortisol decline (p < 0.01), self-reported stress recovery, and maintained dopaminergic sensitivity compared with both abstinent controls and high-frequency binge users. Animated content was associated with lower real-world interpersonal distress scores and higher reported satisfaction with fantasy-reality boundaries. Findings support a non-linear, dose-dependent model in which moderated, intentional use functions as a low-cost regulatory behavior rather than a net harm.

Key Findings

  • Participants in the moderated group exhibited steeper diurnal cortisol slopes and lower evening residual stress than both abstainers and heavy users.
  • Preference for animated / illustrated erotic media correlated with reduced intrusive real-life sexual thoughts and higher comfort with fantasy as a discrete category.
  • No measurable decline in partnered sexual desire or erectile function was observed in the moderated cohort; slight increases in reported sexual mindfulness were noted.
  • Self-reported guilt was highest in the high-frequency group and lowest in the moderated animated-preferring subgroup, suggesting that content style and intentional framing moderate affective outcomes.

Discussion

These results align with emerging models of “adaptive erotic regulation.” When use remains intentional, time-bounded, and oriented toward stylized rather than hyper-realistic stimuli, the activity appears to serve a homeostatic function similar to other low-stakes sensory rewards. The data do not support blanket abstinence recommendations for individuals who already experience benefits from moderated engagement.

Further research is underway examining long-term maintenance of these effects and the role of intentional edging or prolonged arousal states within safe parameters.
